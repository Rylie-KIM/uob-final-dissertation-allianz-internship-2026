## 🇰🇷 한국어 요약

### 1. 문제 정의 — "공정성의 잘못된 측정"

표준 공정성 기준들(인구통계학적 동등성, equalized odds, 보정)이 실제 의사결정에서 왜 실패하는가?

논문의 핵심 논지: **기존 공정성 기준은 의사결정 경계(margin)에서의 행동이 아니라 경계에서 멀리 떨어진 곳에서의 행동을 평가한다**. 이것이 "inframarginality 문제(내부경계 문제)"다.

**예시 (당뇨병 스크리닝, Figure 2):**
- 아시아인의 위험 분포: 전반적으로 낮고 좁게 분포
- 백인의 위험 분포: 더 넓고 오른쪽 꼬리가 큰 분포
- 단일 임계값 t를 사용하면: 아시아인 집단의 대부분이 임계값 훨씬 아래 → 저위험 (스크리닝 대상 아님), 반면 고위험 백인은 스크리닝 대상
- 결과: demographic parity 위반 (두 집단의 스크리닝 비율이 다름)
- **하지만:** 이 정책이 유틸리티를 극대화하는 최적 정책. Inframarginality란 분포 차이가 임계값 아래/위에서 왜 다른지 설명

### 2. 핵심 기술 개념 및 수식

#### 2-1. Inframarginality 문제 (Section 2)

보호 속성 $A$, 위험 점수 $R$, 임계값 $t$인 단일 임계값 정책에서:

$$\pi_a(t) = P(Y=1 \mid R \geq t, A=a) = \frac{P(R \geq t \mid Y=1, A=a) \cdot P(Y=1 \mid A=a)}{P(R \geq t \mid A=a)}$$

집단 $A=0$과 $A=1$의 위험 분포가 다를 때, 동일한 임계값 $t$가 집단별로 다른 선택을 의미한다. 이것이 demographic parity 위반의 수학적 원인.

**핵심:** 단일 임계값은 집단 분포의 임계값 *아래*에 있는 사람들의 혼합 구성에 민감 — 그들은 의사결정에 실제로 영향을 받지 않는데도 집단 간 비율 계산에 영향을 미침.

#### 2-2. Theorem 9 — 분류 패리티의 불가능성

**Theorem 9:** 거의 모든 위험 분포에 대해, 유틸리티 극대화 단일 임계값 정책은 인구통계학적 동등성을 위반하며, 이를 만족하는 정책은 유틸리티 극대화 정책보다 엄격히 낮은 유틸리티를 달성한다.

더 강하게: **equalized FPR 위반도 불가피하다**. Theorem 9는 보다 정확하게:

$$P(D=1 \mid A=0, Y=0) \neq P(D=1 \mid A=1, Y=0)$$

이 거의 모든 위험 분포에서 유틸리티 극대화 임계값에서 성립함을 보인다 (여기서 $D$는 결정 변수).

**증명 스케치:** 위험 분포가 집단별로 다르면, 최적 임계값 $t^*_a$도 집단별로 다름. 단일 임계값 사용은 한 집단에서 over-intervention, 다른 집단에서 under-intervention을 낳음 → FPR 불균형.

#### 2-3. Theorem 10 — "공정성 통한 무지"의 실패

**Theorem 10:** 보호 속성 $A$가 결과 $Y$에 대해 예측력이 있을 때 ($R$ 통제 시에도), **보호 속성을 무시하는 어떤 정책도 유틸리티 극대화를 달성할 수 없다**.

"Fairness through unawareness" — 모델 입력에서 A를 제외 — 가 Pareto 비효율임을 수학적으로 증명.

결론: A를 사용하지 않으면 일부 집단에서 좋은 결정을 내릴 기회를 놓침. A를 적절히 사용하는 것이 집단 모두에게 더 좋은 결과를 낼 수 있음.

#### 2-4. 외부효과 존재 시 공정성 제약 (Section 4)

공정성 제약이 도입될 때 발생하는 Pareto 비효율성:

**Proposition 15:** Pareto-효율적 정책들은 집단별로 다른 임계값을 사용하는 **다중 임계값 정책(multiple-threshold policies)**의 형태를 취한다:

$$D_a(r) = \mathbb{I}[r \geq t_a], \quad t_0 \neq t_1$$

단일 임계값 공정성 기준(demographic parity, equalized FPR 등)을 강제하면 다중 임계값 정책 공간에서 Pareto 전선을 벗어남 → 모든 집단이 더 나빠질 수 있음.

#### 2-5. Theorem 11 — 경로별 공정성의 실패

**Theorem 11:** 경로별 공정성(path-specific fairness)이 최적 정책과 일치하려면, 거의 모든 위험 분포에서 결정이 상수여야 함 (즉, 모든 개인에게 동일한 결정). 이는 사실상 정보 사용을 포기.

#### 2-6. Theorem 17 — 반사실적 equalized odds의 비효율성

**Theorem 17:** 반사실적 equalized odds(counterfactual equalized odds)와 조건부 주요 공정성(conditional principal fairness)은 강하게 Pareto 지배됨 — 이를 만족하면서 모든 집단에게 더 좋은 결과를 내는 정책이 존재함.

#### 2-7. "공정성의 전진 방향" (Section 5)

논문의 결론적 제안: **결과 지향 공정성(outcome-oriented) 대신 과정 지향 공정성(process-oriented)**으로 전환.

- 결과 지향: "집단별 오차율이 같아야 한다" — 외부 효과로 인해 Pareto 비효율
- 과정 지향: "의사결정 과정이 집단에 관계없이 동일한 합리적 기준을 적용하는가?"

보정(calibration)이 과정 지향 공정성의 자연스러운 구현: 동일한 점수 → 동일한 개입 확률, 집단 무관.

### 5. 코드/아키텍처 함의

`test/builds/02_loop_detection_framework/loop_detector.py`에서 Inframarginality 측정:

```python
def inframarginality_test(df, score_col, label_col, group_col, threshold=0.5):
    """
    P24 Section 2 기반: 임계값 근방에서 집단별 구성 분석
    집단별 score 분포의 임계값 근방 KL divergence 측정
    """
    groups = df[group_col].unique()
    
    # 임계값 근방 ±0.1 구간에서 집단별 기저율 비교
    margin_mask = (df[score_col] > threshold - 0.1) & (df[score_col] < threshold + 0.1)
    
    marginal_fraud_rates = {}
    for g in groups:
        group_margin = df[margin_mask & (df[group_col]==g)]
        if len(group_margin) > 0:
            marginal_fraud_rates[g] = group_margin[label_col].mean()
    
    # 임계값 근방의 집단별 기저율 차이 = inframarginality 지표
    rates = list(marginal_fraud_rates.values())
    inframarginality = max(rates) - min(rates) if rates else 0
    
    return {
        'inframarginality_score': inframarginality,
        'marginal_fraud_rates_by_group': marginal_fraud_rates,
        'single_threshold_adequate': inframarginality < 0.05
    }
```

`test/builds/05_randomisation_strategy/randomisation_policy.py`에서 Proposition 15 구현:

```python
def compute_pareto_efficient_thresholds(group_score_distributions, utility_fn):
    """
    P24 Proposition 15: Pareto-효율적 다중 임계값 정책 탐색
    각 집단에 대해 독립적으로 최적 임계값 계산
    """
    group_thresholds = {}
    for group, score_dist in group_score_distributions.items():
        # 집단별 유틸리티 최적 임계값
        t_opt = argmax over t of E[utility_fn(score, t) | group]
        group_thresholds[group] = t_opt
    return group_thresholds  # Proposition 15: 이것이 Pareto 전선
```

## 🇬🇧 English Summary

### 1. Problem — Mismeasurement of Fairness Criteria

The paper's central argument: standard fairness criteria (demographic parity, equalized odds, equalised FPR/FNR) are fundamentally mismeasured because they assess model behaviour **away from the decision margin**, not at it. This is the "inframarginality problem."

The decisive example (diabetes screening, Figure 2): Asian-American and White patients have different risk distributions. The utility-maximising single-threshold policy inevitably produces different screening rates for the two groups — violating demographic parity. But the mismatch is not at the margin (where decisions are actually contested); it arises from the different shapes of the distributions far from the threshold. Penalising this mismatch punishes a policy that is actually better for both groups.

The implication for fraud detection: SFP loops widen the gap between group-level risk distributions across model versions, deepening inframarginality with each retraining cycle.

### 2. Core Technical Concepts and Formulae

#### 2-1. Inframarginality (Section 2)

For a single-threshold policy $D_a(r) = \mathbb{I}[r \geq t]$, the screening rate for group $a$ is:

$$s_a(t) = P(R \geq t \mid A=a)$$

When the risk distribution $P(R \mid A=a)$ differs across groups, the same threshold $t$ produces different screening rates even if the marginal individual at the threshold has identical risk in both groups. The screening rate is determined by the entire distribution below and above $t$, not just the distribution at $t$.

This makes demographic parity and equalized FPR sensitive to the composition of people far from the decision boundary — precisely those who are not affected by the decision.

#### 2-2. Theorem 9 — Incompatibility of Classification Parity

**Theorem 9:** For almost every risk distribution, the utility-maximising threshold policy violates demographic parity and equalised FPR. Any policy satisfying these criteria achieves strictly lower utility.

The proof uses the first-order condition for utility maximisation: the optimal threshold equates the ratio of benefit to harm at the margin, which depends on the group-level base rate $\pi_a = P(Y=1 \mid A=a)$. When $\pi_0 \neq \pi_1$, the optimal thresholds $t^*_0 \neq t^*_1$, and any single threshold violates one group's optimality condition, producing different FPR values.

#### 2-3. Theorem 10 — Failure of Fairness through Unawareness

**Theorem 10:** When protected attribute $A$ has predictive power for outcome $Y$ (even conditional on the risk score $R$), no blind policy — one that ignores $A$ — is utility-maximising.

In other words, using group information is not discrimination; it is efficient and, paradoxically, can be more equitable. A policy that ignores predictively valid group information necessarily makes worse decisions for some group.

#### 2-4. Pareto-Efficient Policies Under Externalities (Section 4)

**Proposition 15:** Pareto-efficient policies take the form of multiple-threshold policies: $D_a(r) = \mathbb{I}[r \geq t_a]$ with group-specific thresholds $t_a$.

Imposing single-threshold fairness constraints (demographic parity, equalized FPR) forces the policy off the Pareto frontier. There exists a Pareto improvement that benefits all groups but violates the fairness constraint — the constraint is costly.

#### 2-5. Theorem 11 — Path-Specific Fairness Failure

**Theorem 11:** For almost every risk distribution, path-specific fairness constraints require decisions to be constant (the same for all individuals) — which is tantamount to ignoring all information.

Path-specific fairness, which tries to distinguish "legitimate" from "illegitimate" causal pathways, is so restrictive that it eliminates any meaningful use of predictive information.

#### 2-6. Theorem 17 — Counterfactual Fairness Failure

**Theorem 17:** Counterfactual equalized odds and conditional principal fairness are strongly Pareto dominated — any policy satisfying these criteria is dominated by a policy that is better for all groups simultaneously.

#### 2-7. A Path Forward: Process-Oriented Fairness (Section 5)

The paper's constructive proposal: shift from outcome-oriented to process-oriented fairness.

- **Outcome-oriented:** "Error rates must be equal across groups" — leads to Pareto losses.
- **Process-oriented:** "The decision process applies the same rational criterion regardless of group membership" — implemented naturally via calibration.

Calibration as process fairness: if $P(Y=1 \mid R=r, A=a) = r$ for all $a$, then the same score means the same risk regardless of group membership, and a uniform threshold policy treats equal risks equally.

### 3. Experimental Results

#### 3-1. Diabetes Screening (Figure 2)

When Asian-American and White risk distributions differ:
- Utility-optimal single threshold: unequal screening rates (demographic parity violation)
- Demographic parity-constrained threshold: over-screens Asian-Americans, under-screens White patients → net utility loss

The fairness constraint transfers harm from one group to another without eliminating it — and creates net inefficiency.

#### 3-2. Calibration vs. Classification Parity (Figure 3)

Group-blind scores fail calibration within groups; Bayes-optimal group-aware scores automatically calibrate. Using protected attributes to achieve optimal calibration is not discriminatory by the process-oriented standard — it is informative.

| **Build 06: Causal Mitigation** | Section 5 process-oriented fairness: IPW reweighting restores calibration — the same fraud score means the same risk regardless of group. This is the mechanistic implementation of the paper's "path forward." |

#### 4-2. SFP Loop as Inframarginality Amplifier

The SFP loop specifically worsens inframarginality:
1. v1 over-investigates high-risk-postcode claims → inflated fraud labels in that group
2. v2 trained on inflated labels assigns higher scores to that group → group score distribution shifts right
3. The gap between group distributions widens with each model version
4. Inframarginality grows: more of the population of the targeted group sits well above the threshold, driving FPR gaps
5. Theorem 9 guarantees this generates equalized odds violations that no single threshold can resolve

The SFP loop is thus not only a selection bias problem (P1) and a feedback problem (P23) but also an inframarginality problem (P24) — all three perspectives are needed for a complete diagnosis.

### 5. Limitations

- **Externality assumption:** The Pareto analysis assumes decisions on one individual do not affect others. In insurance, fraud investigations have deterrence externalities — the optimal policy under externalities is more complex.
- **Known distribution assumption:** Theorems assume the group-level risk distributions are known. The SFP loop corrupts these distributions — a prerequisite is restoring an unbiased estimate (Build 06).
- **Binary decision focus:** The paper analyses binary threshold policies. Insurance fraud triage involves graded interventions (enhanced scrutiny, full investigation, immediate rejection) which require extension of the multiple-threshold framework.
- **Calibration as process fairness:** The proposal to use calibration as the process-oriented standard is compelling but does not fully escape Chouldechova's impossibility — a calibrated model still inevitably generates unequal error rates when base rates differ.
