## 🇰🇷 한국어 요약

### 1. 문제 정의 — 정규화 편향 문제

인과 추론에서 핵심 목표는 저차원 처리 효과 파라미터 $\theta_0$를 추정하는 것이다. 그러나 혼란 변수 $X$의 차원이 높을 때, 성향 점수(propensity score) $m_0(X) = E[D|X]$와 결과 회귀 $g_0(X) = E[Y|D,X]$를 머신러닝으로 추정하면 **정규화 편향(regularisation bias)**이 발생한다.

**핵심 문제 (§1.1):** 단순 플러그인 추정기는:

$$\hat{\theta}_0 = \left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i \in I} D_i^2\right)^{-1} \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i \in I} D_i(Y_i - \hat{g}_0(X_i)),$$

여기서 $|\sqrt{n}(\hat{\theta}_0 - \theta_0)| \to_P \infty$. 즉 $\sqrt{n}$-일치성을 잃는다.

**왜?** 편향 항이:
$$b = (\text{E}D_i^2)^{-1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i \in I} m_0(X_i)(g_0(X_i) - \hat{g}_0(X_i)) + o_P(1)$$

$\hat{g}_0$의 수렴 속도가 $n^{-\varphi_g}$ ($\varphi_g < 1/2$)이면 이 항이 발산한다. LASSO, 랜덤 포레스트, 신경망 등 모든 ML 방법에서 발생하는 근본적인 문제다.

### 2. 해결책 — Double/Debiased ML (DML)의 두 핵심 요소

#### 2-1. Neyman 직교성 (Definition 2.1 — 핵심)

목적: score 함수 $\psi(W; \theta, \eta)$가 nuisance 파라미터 $\eta$ 추정 오류에 **1차적으로 둔감**하도록 만든다.

Neyman 직교 조건 (식 2.3):

$$\partial_\eta \text{E}_P \psi(W; \theta_0, \eta_0)[\eta - \eta_0] = 0, \quad \text{for all } \eta \in \mathcal{T}_N.$$

**직관:** $\psi$의 Gateaux 도함수가 0이 되면, $\hat{\eta}_0 \neq \eta_0$이어도 $\psi$의 기댓값이 거의 변하지 않는다. 이것이 정규화 편향을 제거하는 메커니즘이다.

부분선형회귀(Partially Linear Regression, PLR) 모형에서의 Neyman 직교 score (식 4.3):

$$\psi(W; \theta, \eta) = (Y - D\theta - g(X))(D - m(X)), \quad \eta = (g, m).$$

편향 항이 이제 **두 추정 오류의 곱**에 의존한다:

$$b^* = (\text{E}V^2)^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i \in I}(\hat{m}_0(X_i) - m_0(X_i))(\hat{g}_0(X_i) - g_0(X_i)),$$

각 추정기가 $n^{-1/4}$ 속도로 수렴하면 곱은 $n^{-1/2}$으로 충분히 빠르다 ($\sqrt{s^g/N}\sqrt{s^m/N} \ll N^{-1/2} \Leftrightarrow s^g s^m \ll N$).

#### 2-2. 교차 피팅 (Cross-Fitting) (Definition 3.1 — DML1)

K-폴드 교차 피팅 알고리즘:

1. 데이터를 K개 폴드 $\{I_k\}_{k=1}^K$로 랜덤 분할
2. 각 폴드 $k$에 대해: $I_k^c = \{1,\ldots,N\} \setminus I_k$ (보완 집합)에서 $\hat{\eta}_{0,k}$ 추정
3. $I_k$ 위에서 $\hat{\theta}_{0,k}$을 $\mathbb{E}_{n,k}[\psi(W; \hat{\theta}_{0,k}, \hat{\eta}_{0,k})] = 0$의 해로 정의
4. 집계: $\tilde{\theta}_0 = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \hat{\theta}_{0,k}$

핵심: $\hat{\eta}_{0,k}$는 $I_k$에 속하지 않는 데이터로 학습되므로, 평가 시점에 독립적이다 → 과적합 편향 제거.

**Remark 3.1:** $K=4$ 또는 $K=5$가 실무에서 권장된다. $K=2$보다 성능이 좋다.

#### 2-3. 주요 정리 (Theorem 3.1)

Assumptions 3.1, 3.2 하에서 DML1, DML2 추정기는:

$$\sqrt{N}\,\sigma^{-1}(\tilde{\theta}_0 - \theta_0) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^N \bar{\psi}(W_i) + O_P(\rho_N) \rightsquigarrow N(0, I_d),$$

여기서 잔차 크기는:
$$\rho_N := N^{-1/2} + r_N + r'_N + N^{1/2}\lambda_N + N^{1/2}\lambda'_N \lesssim \delta_N.$$

**실무적 함의:** ML 추정기가 $o(N^{-1/4})$ 속도로 수렴하기만 하면 처리 효과 추정기는 $\sqrt{N}$-일치성을 가진다. LASSO, 랜덤 포레스트, 부스팅, 신경망 모두 이 조건을 만족할 수 있다.

#### 2-4. ATE 추정을 위한 Neyman 직교 Score (§5.1, 식 5.3)

평균 처리 효과(ATE) 추정:

$$\psi(W; \theta, \eta) := (g(1, X) - g(0, X)) + \frac{D(Y - g(1,X))}{m(X)} - \frac{(1-D)(Y - g(0,X))}{1 - m(X)} - \theta,$$

여기서 $\eta = (g, m)$, $g(d, X) = E[Y|D=d, X]$, $m(X) = E[D|X]$. 이 score는 Neyman 직교이며 **세미파라미터 효율 경계**를 달성한다 (Hahn 1998).

### 5. 한계 (논문 명시)

- **$\mathcal{D}(\theta)$의 안정성 가정:** 교차 피팅이 overfitting 편향을 제거하지만, 데이터 생성 과정 자체가 비정상(non-stationary)이면 이론 보장이 약해진다.
- **$o(N^{-1/4})$ 속도 요구:** LASSO와 랜덤 포레스트는 이 조건을 충족하지만, 비정규 데이터에서는 보장이 어렵다.
- **이진 처리 변수:** `investigated`는 이진 변수지만 성향 점수의 극단값(0에 가깝거나 1에 가까운 경우) 문제가 발생할 수 있다. 실무에서 propensity trimming (예: [0.05, 0.95]) 필요.
- **교란 없음(Unconfoundedness) 가정:** 관측되지 않은 혼란 변수가 있으면 DML도 무력화된다. Allianz에서 `model_v1_score` 자체가 누락 변수(unmeasured confounder)일 가능성.
- **Donsker 조건 위반:** ML 추정기는 고차원에서 Donsker 조건을 위반 → 교차 피팅이 이를 우회하는 핵심 이유.

## 🇬🇧 English Summary

### 1. Problem — Regularisation Bias in Causal Inference with ML

When estimating a low-dimensional treatment effect parameter $\theta_0$ in the presence of high-dimensional confounders $X$, the natural approach is to estimate nuisance parameters $\eta_0 = (g_0, m_0)$ (outcome regression and propensity score) using ML methods and plug them into estimating equations. This naive plug-in strategy fails: regularisation bias in estimating $\eta_0$ contaminates the treatment effect estimate at a rate faster than $N^{-1/2}$, invalidating standard inference.

The bias term in the partially linear regression (PLR) model $Y = D\theta_0 + g_0(X) + U$ is:

$$b = (\text{E}D_i^2)^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i \in I} m_0(X_i)(g_0(X_i) - \hat{g}_0(X_i)) + o_P(1),$$

which diverges whenever $\hat{g}_0$ converges at a sub-$\sqrt{n}$ rate (as all regularised ML estimators do in high dimensions).

---

### 2. The DML Solution — Two Ingredients

#### 2-1. Neyman Orthogonality (Definition 2.1, equation 2.3)

A score function $\psi(W; \theta, \eta)$ satisfies Neyman orthogonality at $(\theta_0, \eta_0)$ if:

$$\partial_\eta \text{E}_P\psi(W; \theta_0, \eta_0)[\eta - \eta_0] = 0, \quad \text{for all } \eta \in \mathcal{T}_N.$$

This is the Gateaux derivative condition: the score is **first-order insensitive** to perturbations of the nuisance parameter around its true value. When this holds, the residual bias term $b^*$ depends only on the **product** of estimation errors in $\hat{m}_0$ and $\hat{g}_0$:

$$b^* = (\text{E}V^2)^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_i(\hat{m}_0(X_i) - m_0(X_i))(\hat{g}_0(X_i) - g_0(X_i)).$$

If both converge at rate $N^{-1/4}$, the product converges at $N^{-1/2}$ — fast enough for valid inference. For the PLR model, the Neyman orthogonal score is:

$$\psi(W; \theta, \eta) = (Y - D\theta - g(X))(D - m(X)), \quad \eta = (g, m).$$

This can be derived by partialling out $X$ from both $Y$ and $D$ (the Robinson 1988 approach), giving residuals $\tilde{Y} = Y - \ell_0(X)$ and $\tilde{D} = D - m_0(X)$, then regressing $\tilde{Y}$ on $\tilde{D}$.

#### 2-2. Cross-Fitting (Definition 3.1 — DML1)

Cross-fitting eliminates bias due to overfitting when the same sample is used for both nuisance estimation and target parameter estimation:

1. Randomly partition $[N]$ into $K$ folds $\{I_k\}_{k=1}^K$, each of size $n = N/K$.
2. For each fold $k$: estimate $\hat{\eta}_{0,k}$ using observations in the complement $I_k^c = \{1,\ldots,N\} \setminus I_k$.
3. Construct $\hat{\theta}_{0,k}$ from the estimating equation on fold $I_k$ using $\hat{\eta}_{0,k}$.
4. Aggregate: $\tilde{\theta}_0 = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \hat{\theta}_{0,k}$.

The key property: since $\hat{\eta}_{0,k}$ is estimated on data that does not include $I_k$, the plug-in term $\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i \in I_k} V_i(\hat{g}_0(X_i) - g_0(X_i))$ has conditional mean zero, removing the remainder bias $c^*$ that otherwise diverges.

#### 2-3. Main Result — Theorem 3.1

Under Assumptions 3.1 (near-orthogonality, score regularity) and 3.2 (quality of nuisance estimators), the DML estimator $\tilde{\theta}_0$ satisfies:

$$\sqrt{N}\,\sigma^{-1}(\tilde{\theta}_0 - \theta_0) \rightsquigarrow N(0, I_d),$$

uniformly over a broad class of probability measures. The remainder term satisfies $\rho_N \lesssim \delta_N = o(1)$, which requires only that nuisance parameters converge at rate $o(N^{-1/4})$ — achievable by lasso, random forests, gradient boosting, and neural networks under mild sparsity/smoothness conditions.

#### 2-4. ATE Orthogonal Score (§5.1, equations 5.3–5.4)

For the interactive model $Y = g_0(D, X) + U$, $D = m_0(X) + V$, the Neyman orthogonal score for the ATE is:

$$\psi(W; \theta, \eta) = (g(1,X) - g(0,X)) + \frac{D(Y - g(1,X))}{m(X)} - \frac{(1-D)(Y-g(0,X))}{1-m(X)} - \theta.$$

This is the augmented inverse propensity weighted (AIPW) estimator, which is both Neyman orthogonal and achieves the semiparametric efficiency bound of Hahn (1998). The nuisance parameter is $\eta = (g, m)$.

### 3. Experimental Results (§6)

The paper demonstrates DML on three empirical examples:

**Example 1 — Pennsylvania Reemployment Bonus (§6.1):**
- Binary treatment (bonus scheme assignment), outcome (log unemployment duration).

#### 4-3. Practical Implementation Notes

The DML approach for Build 04 in `intervention_analysis.py`:

```python
# Neyman orthogonal score for PLR (equation 4.3):
# psi(W; theta, eta) = (Y - D*theta - g(X)) * (D - m(X))
# 
# Equivalent to Robinson (1988) partialling-out:
# 1. Regress Y on X using ML -> residuals Y_tilde = Y - E[Y|X]
# 2. Regress D on X using ML -> residuals D_tilde = D - E[D|X]  
# 3. Regress Y_tilde on D_tilde -> coefficient is theta_0
#
# Cross-fitting ensures E[V_i * (g_hat(X_i) - g_0(X_i))] = 0

from econml.dml import LinearDML
import lightgbm as lgb

est = LinearDML(
    model_y=lgb.LGBMClassifier(n_estimators=300, 
                                num_leaves=31,
                                learning_rate=0.05),
    model_t=lgb.LGBMClassifier(n_estimators=300,
                                num_leaves=31, 
                                learning_rate=0.05),
    discrete_treatment=True,
    cv=5,           # 5-fold cross-fitting per DML recommendation
    random_state=42
)
est.fit(Y, D, X=X_confounders, W=None)

# ATE with 95% confidence interval (uniformly valid)
theta_ate = est.ate(X_confounders)
theta_ci = est.ate_interval(X_confounders, alpha=0.05)
```

### 5. Limitations

- **Unconfoundedness assumption:** DML cannot handle unmeasured confounders. In the Allianz pipeline, if `model_v1_score` encodes information not present in the observed feature columns $X$ (e.g., adjuster notes, phone call information), the no-unmeasured-confounders assumption fails.
- **Rate requirement $o(N^{-1/4})$:** Achievable in practice but requires careful hyperparameter tuning of LightGBM; cross-validated selection of regularisation is essential.
- **Binary outcome complications:** When $Y$ is binary (`fraud_label`), the linear DML score is approximately valid but not fully efficient; the non-linear interactive model DML (§5) is preferred.
- **Propensity score overlap:** If the Allianz model produces near-deterministic investigation decisions (very high or low scores drive all assignments), the propensity score $m(X)$ approaches 0 or 1, causing extreme weights. Trimming at [0.05, 0.95] is advisable.
- **Stationarity:** The DML theory assumes i.i.d. data. Temporal structure in claims data (seasonal fraud patterns, model version deployment dates) requires time-aware cross-fitting splits to avoid leakage.

## 🎯 Interview Talking Points (P16-specific)

| Prompt | Use P16 to say... |
|--------|-------------------|
| *"Why can't you just fit a propensity score model and use inverse probability weighting?"* | "Chernozhukov et al. (Econometrics Journal 2018) show that plugging in an ML-estimated propensity score introduces regularisation bias. The bias term scales as $(\hat{m} - m_0)(\hat{g} - g_0)$, which diverges when either nuisance estimator converges slower than $N^{-1/2}$ — as all regularised methods do. DML's two fixes are Neyman orthogonality (making the score first-order insensitive to nuisance error) and cross-fitting (removing overfitting bias). Together they restore $\sqrt{N}$-consistent ATE estimation even when propensity scores come from LightGBM." |
| *"How do you implement DML in your Build 04 intervention analysis?"* | "I use econml's `LinearDML` with LightGBM nuisance models and 5-fold cross-fitting. For each fold, I train the propensity score $P(\text{investigated}=1|X)$ and outcome regression $E[\text{fraud}|D,X]$ on the out-of-fold data, then evaluate the Neyman orthogonal score on the held-out fold. The ATE estimate is the average across folds with a uniformly valid 95% confidence interval from Theorem 3.1." |
| *"What is Neyman orthogonality and why does it matter?"* | "The Neyman orthogonal condition (Definition 2.1, equation 2.3) requires the Gateaux derivative of the score function with respect to the nuisance parameter to vanish at the true parameter value. Intuitively, this means a small error in estimating the propensity score has zero first-order effect on the treatment effect estimate — it only enters through second-order terms. The partially linear score $\psi = (Y - D\theta - g(X))(D - m(X))$ achieves this by centering both $Y$ and $D$ on their conditional expectations." |
| *"What is cross-fitting and why is it necessary?"* | "Cross-fitting partitions the data into K folds, estimates nuisance parameters on each fold's complement, and evaluates the score on the fold itself. Without this, if the same data is used to estimate both the nuisance and the target parameter, overfitting in the nuisance estimate introduces a remainder term that diverges. With K=5 cross-fitting, Theorem 3.1 guarantees the remainder is $o(N^{-1/2})$ under mild ML convergence conditions — Figure 2 in the paper shows the bias vanishing completely." |
| *"What is the treatment effect you're trying to estimate in Build 04?"* | "The ATE of investigation on fraud discovery: $\theta_0 = E[Y(\text{investigated}=1) - Y(\text{investigated}=0)]$. This is the causal effect of assigning an investigation to a claim on whether fraud is ultimately detected. Using the interactive model score (equation 5.3), which includes both the outcome regression $g(D, X)$ and propensity $m(X)$, I get a doubly robust estimate: valid if either the outcome model or the propensity model is correct." |
| *"How do DML propensity scores connect to your IPW debiasing in Build 06?"* | "Build 06 uses DoWhy to reweight training data from $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ back toward the unbiased distribution $\mathcal{D}_0$. The IPW weights are $w_i = 1/\hat{m}(X_i)$ for investigated claims and $1/(1-\hat{m}(X_i))$ for non-investigated. If $\hat{m}$ is estimated naively (same data, regularised LightGBM), the weights are biased. Cross-fitted $\hat{m}$ from DML gives debiased weights, which is critical because the IPW estimator is highly sensitive to propensity score quality near the extremes." |
| *"Why use 5-fold rather than 2-fold or leave-one-out?"* | "Remark 3.1 in the paper states that moderate K (4 or 5) works better than K=2 in finite samples because larger K provides more observations in $I_k^c$ for estimating high-dimensional nuisance functions. Leave-one-out is computationally prohibitive with LightGBM. The paper recommends DML2 (pooled Jacobian across folds, equation 3.4) over DML1 for more stable behaviour, and this is what `econml.LinearDML` implements by default." |
| *"What are the limits of DML for the Allianz problem?"* | "Two main limits. First, DML assumes unconfoundedness — no unmeasured confounders. In fraud detection, adjusters who reviewed a claim may have soft information (call tone, narrative inconsistency) that drives both investigation and outcome but isn't in our feature columns. This violates the key assumption. Second, the propensity score in Allianz is likely near-degenerate for high-scoring claims (almost certainly investigated), causing extreme weights. I handle this with propensity trimming at [0.05, 0.95] in `intervention_analysis.py`." |
