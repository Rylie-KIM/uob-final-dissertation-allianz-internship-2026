## 🇰🇷 한국어 요약

### 1. 문제 정의 — "Performative Prediction"이란 무엇인가

지도학습은 고정 분포 $\mathcal{D}$에서 i.i.d.로 추출된 데이터로 손실을 최소화한다는 가정 아래 작동한다. 그러나 **예측이 의사결정을 지원할 때**, 배포된 모델 자체가 그 모델이 예측하려는 바로 그 분포를 바꾸어버린다. 이를 논문은 **performative(수행적)**라 부른다.

**핵심 직관 (논문 §1):**
> "A bank predicts that a loan applicant has elevated default risk and sets a high interest rate. In a self-fulfilling prophecy, the high interest rate further increases the customer's default risk."

이 현상은 신용 평가, 교통 예측, 추천 시스템, 주가 예측, **보험 사기 탐지** 등 예측 결과가 인간 행동을 변화시키는 모든 분야에 편재한다 (논문 Appendix A, Table 1 — Security/Insurance 항목 명시).

전통 ERM(경험적 위험 최소화)은 **고정 분포** $\mathcal{D}_0$에서의 손실을 최소화한다:

$$\theta_{\text{SL}} = \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}} [\ell(Z; \theta)].$$

그러나 예측이 수행적일 때, 모델 파라미터 $\theta$를 배포하면 **분포 자체가 $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$로 변한다**. 따라서 올바른 목표는:

$$\text{PR}(\theta) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)} [\ell(Z; \theta)].$$

이것이 **Performative Risk**다. 표준 ERM은 $\mathcal{D}_0$ 위에서 최적화하지만, 실제 배포 후 손실은 $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ 위에서 발생한다. **이 두 값의 차이가 SFP 루프의 수학적 정의**다.

### 2. 핵심 기술 개념

#### 2-1. 두 가지 해 개념 (Definition 2.1, 2.3)

논문은 두 개의 해 개념이 **일반적으로 다르다**는 것을 보인다:

| 개념 | 정의 | 직관 |
|------|------|------|
| **Performative Optimality** ($\theta_{\text{PO}}$) | $\arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)} [\ell(Z; \theta)]$ | 수행적 위험을 최소화하는 진짜 최적 모델 |
| **Performative Stability** ($\theta_{\text{PS}}$) | $\theta_{\text{PS}} = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\text{PS}})} [\ell(Z; \theta)]$ | 재훈련을 반복해도 변하지 않는 고정점 |

- $\theta_{\text{PS}}$는 재훈련의 고정점(fixed point)이다 — 재훈련해도 같은 모델이 반환됨.
- $\theta_{\text{PS}}$는 $\theta_{\text{PO}}$를 보장하지 않는다. 즉, **루프가 안정화된다고 해서 최적이 되는 것이 아니다**.
- 편향된 동전 던지기 예시 (Example 2.2): $\varepsilon \neq 0$이면 $\theta_{\text{PO}} = \frac{\mu}{1-2\varepsilon}$이고 $\theta_{\text{PS}} = \frac{\mu}{1-\varepsilon}$로 서로 다르다.

#### 2-2. $\varepsilon$-민감도 (Definition 3.1)

분포 맵 $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$의 규칙성 조건. $W_1$은 Wasserstein-1 거리(earth mover's distance):

$$W_1(\mathcal{D}(\theta), \mathcal{D}(\theta')) \leq \varepsilon \|\theta - \theta'\|_2.$$

직관: 모델 파라미터가 조금 바뀌면 분포도 그만큼만 바뀐다. $\varepsilon$이 클수록 분포가 모델에 민감하게 반응한다.

#### 2-3. Repeated Risk Minimization (RRM) (Definition 3.3, Theorem 3.5)

실제 ML 운영에서의 재훈련 절차를 공식화:

$$\theta_{t+1} = G(\theta_t) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_t)} [\ell(Z; \theta)].$$

**Theorem 3.5:** 손실 $\ell(z;\theta)$이 $\beta$-jointly smooth (A1)이고 $\gamma$-strongly convex (A2)이며, $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$이 $\varepsilon$-sensitive이고 $\varepsilon < \frac{\gamma}{\beta}$이면:

$$\|\theta_t - \theta_{\text{PS}}\|_2 \leq \delta \quad \text{for all} \quad t \geq \left(1 - \varepsilon\frac{\beta}{\gamma}\right)^{-1} \log\!\left(\frac{\|\theta_0 - \theta_{\text{PS}}\|_2}{\delta}\right).$$

즉, **선형 속도**로 수행적 안정점에 수렴한다. 이때 수렴을 보장하는 **필요충분조건**은 강볼록성(strong convexity) — 단순 볼록성으로는 부족하다 (Proposition 3.6).

#### 2-4. Repeated Gradient Descent (RGD) (Definition 3.7, Theorem 3.8)

RRM은 정확한 최적화를 요구하지만, 실용적으로는 한 스텝의 경사하강으로 근사한다:

$$\theta_{t+1} = G_{\text{gd}}(\theta_t) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \Pi_\Theta\!\left(\theta_t - \eta \,\mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_t)}[\nabla_\theta \ell(Z; \theta_t)]\right).$$

수렴 조건: $\varepsilon < \frac{\gamma}{(\beta+\gamma)(1+1.5\eta\beta)}$이고 $\eta \leq \frac{2}{\beta+\gamma}$.

#### 2-5. 안정점과 최적점의 거리 (Theorem 4.3)

$$\|\theta_{\text{PO}} - \theta_{\text{PS}}\|_2 \leq \frac{2L_z \varepsilon}{\gamma},$$

$\varepsilon$이 작을수록 두 해 개념이 근접한다 — **분포 민감도 $\varepsilon$이 SFP 루프 왜곡의 크기를 결정**한다.

### 5. 한계 (논문 §6, Proposition 3.6)

- **분포 맵 $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$은 알려지지 않음** — 실무에서 추정이 어렵다. 논문은 $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$이 의사결정자에게 알려지지 않은 경우를 가정한다.
- **강볼록성 필요** — 단순 볼록 손실로는 RRM이 수렴하지 않을 수 있다 (Proposition 3.6).
- **선형/로지스틱 회귀 가정** — 실험이 선형 classifier에 국한. LightGBM 같은 비선형 모델에서 $\varepsilon$-sensitivity가 성립하는지 별도 검증 필요.
- **전략적 반응의 즉각성 가정** — 실제 사기꾼은 느리게 적응할 수 있으며, 시간 지연(lag)이 이론과 실제를 벌린다.

## 🇬🇧 English Summary

### 1. Problem — What Is Performative Prediction

Standard supervised learning minimises expected loss over a fixed joint distribution $\mathcal{D}$. When predictions support consequential decisions, however, the deployed model changes the very distribution it was trained to predict. The paper formalises this as **performativity**.

**Lead example (§1):** A bank predicts high default risk and sets a high interest rate, which further increases the customer's actual default risk — a self-fulfilling prophecy. The Appendix (Table 1) explicitly lists fraud detection and insurance as performative settings.

Traditional ERM optimises on a fixed $\mathcal{D}_0$:

$$\theta_{\text{SL}} = \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}}[\ell(Z;\theta)].$$

In the performative setting, deploying $\theta$ induces distribution $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$, so the correct objective is the **Performative Risk**:

$$\text{PR}(\theta) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z;\theta)].$$

The gap between $\text{PR}(\theta)$ and the ERM objective is the mathematical formalisation of the SFP loop.

---

### 2. Key Technical Concepts

#### 2-1. Two Solution Concepts (Definitions 2.1 and 2.3)

| Concept | Definition | Intuition |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Performative Optimality** ($\theta_{\text{PO}}$) | $\arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z;\theta)]$ | True best model accounting for induced distribution |
| **Performative Stability** ($\theta_{\text{PS}}$) | $\theta_{\text{PS}} = \arg\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\text{PS}})}[\ell(Z;\theta)]$ | Fixed point of repeated retraining |

These are **distinct in general**. The biased coin example (Example 2.2) shows $\theta_{\text{PO}} = \frac{\mu}{1-2\varepsilon}$ while $\theta_{\text{PS}} = \frac{\mu}{1-\varepsilon}$ whenever $\varepsilon \neq 0$.

Remark 2.4 gives an equivalent characterisation via the **decoupled performative risk** DPR$(\theta, \theta') \overset{\text{def}}{=} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z;\theta')]$:

$$\theta_{\text{PS}} \text{ stable} \iff \theta_{\text{PS}} = \arg\min_\theta \text{DPR}(\theta_{\text{PS}}, \theta).$$
$$\theta_{\text{PO}} \text{ optimal} \iff \theta_{\text{PO}} = \arg\min_\theta \text{DPR}(\theta, \theta).$$

#### 2-2. $\varepsilon$-Sensitivity (Definition 3.1)

The distribution map $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ is $\varepsilon$-sensitive if, for all $\theta, \theta' \in \Theta$:

$$W_1(\mathcal{D}(\theta), \mathcal{D}(\theta')) \leq \varepsilon\|\theta - \theta'\|_2,$$

where $W_1$ is the Wasserstein-1 (earth mover's) distance. This is the key regularity assumption — it quantifies how strongly the deployed model perturbs the data-generating process. **The parameter $\varepsilon$ is the measure of performativity strength in the Allianz context.**

#### 2-3. Repeated Risk Minimisation (RRM) — Theorem 3.5

Starting from any $\theta_0$, define:

$$\theta_{t+1} = G(\theta_t) \overset{\text{def}}{=} \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_t)}[\ell(Z;\theta)].$$

**Main theorem:** If the loss is $\beta$-jointly smooth (A1), $\gamma$-strongly convex (A2), and $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ is $\varepsilon$-sensitive with $\varepsilon < \frac{\gamma}{\beta}$, then $G$ is a contraction and iterates converge to the unique $\theta_{\text{PS}}$ at a linear rate:

$$\|G(\theta) - G(\theta')\|_2 \leq \varepsilon\frac{\beta}{\gamma}\|\theta - \theta'\|_2.$$

Strong convexity is **necessary** — convexity alone does not suffice (Proposition 3.6).

#### 2-4. Repeated Gradient Descent (RGD) — Theorem 3.8

A computationally practical approximation:

$$\theta_{t+1} = \Pi_\Theta\!\left(\theta_t - \eta\,\mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_t)}[\nabla_\theta\ell(Z;\theta_t)]\right).$$

Convergence holds if $\varepsilon < \frac{\gamma}{(\beta+\gamma)(1+1.5\eta\beta)}$ and step size $\eta \leq \frac{2}{\beta+\gamma}$.

#### 2-5. Distance Between Stable and Optimal Points — Theorem 4.3

$$\|\theta_{\text{PO}} - \theta_{\text{PS}}\|_2 \leq \frac{2L_z\varepsilon}{\gamma}.$$

When $\varepsilon$ is small, the stable and optimal points are close; finding stable points via retraining approximately solves the performative risk minimisation problem.

### 3. Experimental Results (§5, Figures 2–3)

**Setting:** Strategic classification (credit scoring). Individuals manipulate strategic features $x_S$ in response to a logistic regression classifier $f_\theta$. Best response: $x'_S = x_S - \varepsilon\theta_S$. Base distribution: Kaggle credit scoring dataset (class-balanced).

**Figure 2 — Convergence:**
- Small $\varepsilon$ (0.01, 1): RRM converges in ~10 iterations, RGD in ~300.
- Large $\varepsilon$ (100, 1000): both diverge, consistent with theory.

**Figure 3 ($\varepsilon = 80$):**
- Performative risk decreases monotonically from ~0.620 to ~0.610 over 30 iterations.
- Classification accuracy stabilises around 70.0–70.5%.
- Distribution shift (dashed green lines) introduces visible "jumps" at each retraining step.

### 4. Direct Relevance to the Allianz SFP Project

The model score $\theta$ drives investigation decisions; fraud is discovered only where investigations occur; this biased data trains the next model $\theta_{t+1}$. In the paper's language, this pipeline defines $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ — the distribution of fraud labels as a function of the deployed model.

#### 4-2. Code Implications

The key quantity to implement in `loop_detector.py` is an empirical estimate of $\varepsilon$:

```python
# loop_detector.py — Step 1: Temporal Prediction Correlation
def estimate_performative_sensitivity(df, v1_col, v2_col):
    """
    Empirical proxy for varepsilon:
    W_1(D(theta_v1), D(theta_v2)) / ||theta_v1 - theta_v2||_2
    Using score distributions as a proxy for D(theta).
    """
    from scipy.stats import wasserstein_distance
    w1 = wasserstein_distance(df[v1_col], df[v2_col])
    param_diff = np.abs(df[v2_col].mean() - df[v1_col].mean())
    return w1 / (param_diff + 1e-8)
```

### 5. Limitations

- The distribution map $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ is **unknown** to the decision-maker — estimation requires strong assumptions about how model scores translate to investigation rates.
- **Strong convexity is required** for convergence guarantees; LightGBM and XGBoost losses are not generally strongly convex, requiring regularisation.
- Experiments use **linear classifiers** — applicability to tree-based boosting models needs separate verification.
- The theory assumes **instantaneous strategic response**; actual fraudster adaptation (and insurer learning) operates over months, introducing time lags not captured by the model.
- Proposition 4.2 shows PR($\theta$) can be **non-convex even under strong regularity** — direct minimisation of performative risk remains computationally hard.
