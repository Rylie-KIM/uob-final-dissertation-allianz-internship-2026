## 🇰🇷 한국어 요약

### 1. 문제 정의 — "공정성의 비용"

미국 형사사법 시스템에서 알고리즘(COMPAS)이 보석 여부를 결정할 때, 흑인 피고인이 백인 피고인보다 고위험으로 잘못 분류되는 비율이 2배 이상이다. 이를 해결하기 위한 **공정성 제약**을 부과하면, 공공 안전이 감소하는 트레이드오프가 발생한다.

보험 사기 탐지로의 번역:
- "피고인" → 클레임
- "위험 점수" → `model_v1_score` / `v2_score` / `v3_score`
- "인종 그룹" → 클레임 세그먼트 (postcode_risk, product_line, claim_type)
- "구금율 격차" → 세그먼트별 조사율(investigation rate) 격차
- "공공 안전 극대화" → 사기 탐지율 극대화
- "공정성 제약" → 세그먼트별 균등 조사율 요구

### 2. 주요 기술 개념

#### 2-1. 세 가지 공정성 정의

**정의 1 — Statistical Parity (통계적 동등성):**
모든 그룹에서 동일한 비율이 구금(조사)됨:

$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X)] \tag{1}$$

**정의 2 — Conditional Statistical Parity (조건부 통계적 동등성):**
정당한 위험 요인 $\ell(X)$를 통제한 후 그룹 간 조사율이 동일:

$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid \ell(X), g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid \ell(X)] \tag{2}$$

**정의 3 — Predictive Equality (예측 동등성):**
False Positive Rate(FPR)이 그룹 간 동일:

$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid Y = 0, g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid Y = 0] \tag{3}$$

**Calibration (캘리브레이션, 보조 개념):**
동일한 위험 점수를 가진 피고인들의 재범율이 그룹 간 동일:

$$\Pr(Y = 1 \mid s(X), g(X)) = \Pr(Y = 1 \mid s(X)) \tag{4}$$

COMPAS는 캘리브레이션은 만족하지만 (Figure 2: 점수 7에서 흑인 61%, 백인 60% 재범), FPR은 흑인이 더 높다. Kleinberg et al. (2017)은 교정(calibration) + FPR 동등성 + TPR 동등성을 동시에 만족하는 알고리즘은 퇴화한 경우를 제외하면 존재하지 않음을 증명했다.

#### 2-2. 즉각 효용(Immediate Utility) 함수

$$u(d, c) = \mathbb{E}[Yd(X) - cd(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X)(p_{Y|X} - c)] \tag{5, 6}$$

- $Y$: 범죄 저지름 여부 (사기 여부)
- $d(X)$: 구금(조사) 결정 확률
- $c$: 구금(조사) 비용 (예방된 범죄 단위)
- $p_{Y|X} = \Pr(Y=1 \mid X)$: 위험 점수

**해석:** $p_{Y|X} > c$인 개인을 조사하면 양(+) 효용. 최적 무제약 규칙은 $p_{Y|X} \geq c$인 모든 개인을 조사하는 단일 임계값 규칙.

#### 2-3. 핵심 정리 (Theorem 3.2)

$D(p_{Y|X})$가 $[0,1]$ 위에 양의 밀도를 가진다면, 각 공정성 조건 하에서 최적 의사결정 규칙 $d^*$는 다음과 같다:

**(1) 무제약 최적:**

$$d^*(X) = \begin{cases} 1 & p_{Y|X} \geq c \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

**(2) Statistical Parity 조건 하 최적:**

$$d^*(X) = \begin{cases} 1 & p_{Y|X} \geq t_{g(X)} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

여기서 $t_{g(X)} \in [0,1]$은 그룹별 임계값. Predictive Equality 제약도 같은 형태이나 임계값 값이 다름.

**(3) Conditional Statistical Parity 조건 하 최적:**

$$d^*(X) = \begin{cases} 1 & p_{Y|X} \geq t_{g(X), \ell(X)} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

임계값이 그룹과 정당한 속성 모두에 의존.

**핵심 함의:** 무제약 최적은 **단일 임계값** → 모든 개인에게 동일한 기준 적용. 공정성 제약 최적은 **그룹별 다른 임계값** → 무제약과 다르므로 **공정성에는 반드시 비용이 따른다**.

#### 2-4. 한계 수익 조건

Proposition 3.3에 의해, 최적 구금율 $\alpha^*$는 한계 피구금자의 재범 확률이 정확히 $c$가 되는 비율:

$$\frac{d}{d\alpha} \mathbb{E}[Yd_\alpha(X)] = c$$

즉, 비용 $c$가 낮아질수록 최적 구금율이 올라가고, 더 많은 사람을 조사해야 효용이 극대화된다.

### 4. Allianz SFP 연구와의 직접 연관성

#### 4-1. 단기 정확도 vs 장기 형평성의 트레이드오프

## 🇬🇧 English Summary

### 1. Problem — The Cost of Fairness

In US pretrial detention, COMPAS risk scores correlate with race: black defendants are misclassified as high-risk at more than double the rate of white defendants. Applying **fairness constraints** to mitigate this disparity reduces overall accuracy — a measurable cost. The paper formalises this cost as constrained optimisation.

Direct translation to Allianz insurance fraud detection:
- "Defendant" → insurance claim
- "Risk score" → `model_v1/v2/v3_score`
- "Race group" → claim segment (postcode_risk, product_line, claim_type)
- "Detention rate disparity" → investigation rate disparity across segments
- "Maximise public safety" → maximise fraud detection
- "Fairness constraint" → equal investigation rate across segments

### 2. Core Technical Concepts

#### 2-1. Three Fairness Definitions

**Statistical parity:** Equal detention (investigation) rates across groups:
$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X)] \tag{1}$$

**Conditional statistical parity:** Equal rates after controlling for legitimate risk factors $\ell(X)$:
$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid \ell(X), g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid \ell(X)] \tag{2}$$

**Predictive equality:** Equal false positive rates across groups:
$$\mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid Y=0, g(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X) \mid Y=0] \tag{3}$$

**Calibration:** Among individuals with the same risk score, the outcome rate is equal across groups:
$$\Pr(Y=1 \mid s(X), g(X)) = \Pr(Y=1 \mid s(X)) \tag{4}$$

Kleinberg et al. (2017) prove that calibration, equal FPR, and equal TPR cannot all hold simultaneously (except in degenerate cases).

#### 2-2. Immediate Utility

$$u(d, c) = \mathbb{E}[Yd(X) - cd(X)] = \mathbb{E}[d(X)(p_{Y|X} - c)] \tag{5, 6}$$

The optimal unconstrained rule detains (investigates) when $p_{Y|X} \geq c$ — a single uniform threshold.

#### 2-3. Theorem 3.2 — Optimal Decision Rules

Under mild regularity (positive density of $p_{Y|X}$ on [0,1]), the unique optimal rules are:

- **Unconstrained:** Single threshold at $p_{Y|X} = c$
- **Under statistical parity / predictive equality:** Group-specific thresholds $t_{g(X)}$
- **Under conditional statistical parity:** Thresholds $t_{g(X), \ell(X)}$ depending on both group and legitimate factors

Since constrained optima use different thresholds than the unconstrained optimum, **fairness has a cost** — measured in lost utility.

The optimal detention rate $\alpha^*$ satisfies:
$$\frac{d}{d\alpha}\mathbb{E}[Yd_\alpha(X)] = c$$
The marginal detainee has exactly probability $c$ of reoffending.

### 3. Experimental Results — Broward County

**Data:** 3,377 defendants, COMPAS scores 1–10, black/white, 2-year violent recidivism.
**Method:** L1-regularised logistic regression + Platt scaling (AUC 0.75 vs COMPAS 0.73).

**Table 1 — The cost of fairness:**

| Constraint | Low-risk detainees | Estimated increase in violent crime |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Statistical parity | 17% | 9% |
| Predictive equality | 14% | 7% |
| Conditional statistical parity | 10% | 4% |

**Key findings:**
- A single threshold detaining 30% yields 40% black vs 18% white detention rates (violates statistical parity) and 32% vs 14% FPR (violates predictive equality)
- Imposing statistical parity requires releasing high-risk defendants to free up capacity for low-risk ones — a 9% increase in violent crime
- COMPAS scores are calibrated (Figure 2: 61% vs 60% reoffending at score 7) but FPRs still differ due to different risk distributions

**Figure 3 — Calibration insufficient to detect discrimination:**
Adding N(0, 0.5) noise to one group's scores produces calibrated but uninformative predictions — the group never exceeds the detention threshold. Calibration checks are necessary but not sufficient; the algorithm itself must be inspected.

### 4. Direct Relevance to the Allianz SFP Project

#### 4-1. The Short-run / Long-run Trade-off

This paper formalises the core dilemma in Build 05:
- **MaxUtil (unconstrained):** Single high threshold → investigate only top-score claims → maximise short-run fraud detection → worsens SFP loop by never exploring low-score segments
- **Constrained (Statistical Parity across segments):** Segment-specific thresholds → equal investigation rates → short-run efficiency loss → long-run: true fraud rates learned across all segments, SFP loop broken

Theorem 3.2 establishes that **any fairness-constrained optimal algorithm must apply segment-specific thresholds** — this is the mathematical justification for Build 05's ε-greedy randomisation, which allocates investigation capacity to low-scoring claims it would otherwise never reach.

#### 4-2. Build-phase contributions

| Allianz phase | What P13 contributes |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Build 02: Loop Detection** | Step 2 (Label Mechanism Test): if investigations are purely score-driven, the system is applying the unconstrained single-threshold rule — Theorem 3.2's MaxUtil case. Detecting this is the diagnostic for whether a fairness constraint is needed |
| **Build 04: Intervention Analysis** | DiD analysis measuring "change in investigation rates before/after threshold policy change" empirically measures Table 1's fairness cost in Allianz data |
| **Build 05: Randomisation** | ε in ε-greedy is the "fairness budget" — how much short-run efficiency to sacrifice for segment exploration. Thompson Sampling dynamically estimates group-specific thresholds from posterior distributions |
| **Build 03: Unbiased Evaluation** | The unconstrained MaxUtil performance (Theorem 3.2 benchmark) is what IPS-corrected evaluation should compare against |
| **Build 06: Causal Mitigation** | Verify that the IPW-retrained model shifts from single-threshold to segment-specific thresholds — demonstrating that debiased training data changes the effective decision rule |

### 5. Limitations

- **Immediate utility only** — long-run effects of fairness constraints (reduced recidivism, community trust) are excluded. Build 05's randomisation is motivated precisely by these long-run dynamics that this paper sets aside
- **Fixed risk distribution assumption** — $p_{Y|X}$ is treated as exogenous, but in an SFP setting the distribution itself is shaped by past decisions (Liu et al., ICML 2018 addresses this directly)
- **Single decision-maker** — Allianz has multiple actors (investigators, underwriters, external SIU) requiring multi-agent extension
- **Binary decisions only** — real insurance investigations have intensity levels that binary $d(X)$ cannot capture

| *"Why might a single risk threshold cause unfair outcomes across claim types even when the model is well-calibrated?"* | "Figure 2 of this paper illustrates exactly this for COMPAS: the scores are calibrated (same reoffending rate at the same score across groups), yet the false positive rates differ because the underlying risk distributions differ. If motor theft claims have a heavier tail of high fraud scores than windscreen claims, a single investigation threshold will flag proportionally more theft claims as high-risk even at equal calibration. This infra-marginality problem is why Step 4 of my SFPDetector examines segment-specific false positive rates, not just overall model AUC." |
| *"What is the 'price of fairness' in insurance fraud detection?"* | "Following Table 1's methodology, I estimate it by comparing: (a) fraud confirmations per 100 investigations under the current score-only policy, against (b) fraud confirmations under a segment-equalised policy at the same total investigation budget. The difference is the fairness cost in lost fraud detection efficiency. Table 1 shows 4–9% increases in crime for the judicial setting; in Allianz data the equivalent number would be an estimated reduction in fraud confirmed per investigation. Build 04's DiD analysis will measure this directly using model version transitions as quasi-experiments." |
| *"Can you make the model fair without harming overall performance?"* | "Not without additional information, according to this paper. The insurance equivalent: instead of binary investigate/don't-investigate, a tiered system (desktop review → telephone interview → field investigation) lets the model be more conservative overall while covering more segments — this is the Build 05 policy design." |

My framework (Build 05) operationalises this as setting ε in ε-greedy to the level that achieves acceptable segment investigation rate parity, then measuring the utility cost empirically via Build 04. | *"What is 'infra-marginality' and why does it matter for Allianz?"* | "It's the observation (Section 5) that group-level outcome statistics — detention rates, FPR — depend on the full distribution of risk scores above the threshold, not just the threshold itself. Two segments can have identical average fraud rates above the investigation threshold, but if one has a wider risk distribution, its investigation rate looks higher.
